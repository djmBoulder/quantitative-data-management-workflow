# Faculty Overview

**Quantitative Data Management and Workflow for Social Scientists** prepares graduate students to work reproducibly with data before entering or alongside quantitative methods courses. It emphasizes practical research habits in Stata or R: preserving source files, documenting decisions, checking transformations, and producing data that another researcher can use.

Donald Mather, Statistics Consultant, Computing & Research Services, Institute of Behavioral Science, University of Colorado Boulder

## Background And Need

I began statistical consulting at IBS in spring 2023 and kept detailed records of the data, software, and skills involved in each consultation. In summer 2024, I worked with a colleague to use those observations in interviews with approximately six faculty members who teach graduate quantitative methods. A recurring gap was preparation for a reproducible quantitative workflow: students learn statistical methods and often receive code, but may receive little instruction in organizing, preparing, checking, and documenting their data. I developed this course to address that gap.

## Curriculum And Assessment

The course includes orientation, nine technical modules, and a capstone, with an estimated **36 to 48 hours** of work in one primary pathway. Each module has a lesson, parallel Stata and R labs, an assignment, and a competency checklist. Students submit graded work in Stata or R; the dual pathway is optional.

Module 06 and 07 evidence can demonstrate specified operations that do not apply to the capstone. All competencies remain required.

## GitHub And Canvas

GitHub makes the materials accessible for browsing, downloading, and future reference, and supports sharing and collaboration around social science research workflows. Students do not need Git experience or a GitHub account to download the public materials.

The enrolled course will use Canvas for enrollment, the module sequence, submissions, feedback, and grades. Completion runs from registration through seven calendar days before CU's semester grade-submission deadline. Two capstone revisions are allowed; no peer review is required. A passing capstone grade, supported by the required module evidence, records completion. The instructor then notifies the micro registrar to issue the badge. See the [course policies](../syllabus/course-policies.md).
