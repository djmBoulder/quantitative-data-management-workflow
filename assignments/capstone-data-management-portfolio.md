# Capstone Data Management Portfolio Assignment

## Purpose

The capstone portfolio is the final badge evidence for the IBS CU Boulder **Quantitative Data Management and Workflow for Social Scientists** microcredential. You will turn approved source data into a transparent, reproducible, analysis-ready dataset with documentation.

Your goal is to show that another researcher can inspect your files, rerun your workflow, understand your data decisions, and use the resulting dataset for its stated analytic purpose. A statistical model is not required.

## Data Options

Choose one of the following options. Both use the same portfolio requirements, rubric, software pathways, and completion policies.

### Option A: Course-Provided Synthetic Data

Use the `synthetic_gss_like` teaching files:

- `data/synthetic/synthetic_gss_like_person_2024.csv`
- `data/synthetic/synthetic_gss_like_person_2025.csv`
- `data/synthetic/synthetic_gss_like_region_context.csv`
- `data/synthetic/data-dictionary.md`

No separate data proposal is needed. Append the two person-level files and merge the regional context as described below. Module 06 assignment evidence may satisfy reshaping when it is not part of your capstone.

### Option B: Instructor-Approved Student-Selected Data

You may use **publicly distributable data relevant to your own research or intended methods coursework**, with instructor approval before beginning the capstone with those data. Complete the [student-selected data proposal](capstone-data-proposal.md) and submit it in Canvas. The proposal identifies your sources, permission to redistribute them, project scope, and where you will demonstrate the required skills.

The source must explicitly permit public redistribution and the proposed educational use. Record the source's terms, any attribution requirements, and the dataset version or release. Public availability by itself is not sufficient. Private, restricted, confidential, identifiable respondent data, student records, and data whose terms prohibit redistribution are not eligible. Instructor approval cannot waive these restrictions. See the [data policy](../syllabus/course-policies.md#capstone-data-options).

Choose a manageable set of files and variables that supports meaningful cleaning or recoding, missing-data assessment, analytic sample decisions, and quality checks. Define the intended analysis so you can explain what makes the resulting data analysis-ready. Plan a scope consistent with the capstone's estimated 6–8 hours of work and the semester cutoff. If approval is not granted or the project proves unsuitable, use Option A.

Keep approved source files in your local `data/raw/` folder or a clearly documented project folder. Do not replace the course's synthetic teaching files or commit student-selected data to either course repository. All Module 00–09 assignments and guided labs continue to use their assigned teaching data.

## Pathway Options

Choose one primary pathway, or choose the optional dual pathway:

- **Stata pathway:** submit one complete reproducible Stata do-file.
- **R pathway:** submit one complete reproducible R script.
- **Dual Stata/R pathway:** submit both scripts implementing the same major data decisions with the same approved inputs.

The software choice is independent of the data option. All workflows must use relative paths, preserve source files, generate outputs from code, and document major decisions.

## Using The Worked Examples

The Module 10 labs are complete teaching examples. You may use their code as a starting point, but your submission must explain and justify the data decisions in your own workflow. Check every output against your chosen variables and analytic purpose. Replace the example memo with your own 500–900-word response to the prompt below; an unchanged lab-generated memo is not a completed capstone memo.

## Required Capstone Evidence

Submit a complete data management portfolio containing:

1. A reproducible Stata do-file or R script, or both for the optional dual pathway.
2. An analysis-ready dataset generated by the script.
3. A data dictionary or codebook describing source and derived variables.
4. A quality assurance (QA) checklist or report showing checks and their results.
5. A workflow memo explaining decisions, limitations, and rerun instructions.
6. A log or text record of the workflow run.
7. Evidence of cleaning and recoding, missing-data assessment, analytic sample decisions, identifier checks, and observation counts.
8. Append, merge, and reshape evidence as specified in the section below.
9. For Option B, the approved proposal and source documentation. Include permitted copies of the approved input files or the instructor-approved retrieval instructions for the exact versions used, so the instructor can rerun the workflow.

Submit the portfolio in Canvas. Keep student-selected source files and generated datasets, reports, and logs out of the course repositories. Existing Module 06 and 07 submissions can be referenced by Canvas assignment name and exact file or script section rather than uploaded again.

## Where To Demonstrate Reshape, Append, And Merge

All three competencies remain required for the badge. These arrangements identify where the instructor will assess them; they do not waive a competency or replace required module assignments.

| Skill | Option A: synthetic capstone | Option B: approved student-selected data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reshape | Use the Module 06 assignment when reshaping is not part of the capstone. | Reshape and validate when the approved workflow needs it. Otherwise, cite the Module 06 assignment and explain why reshaping does not apply. |
| Append | Append the 2024 and 2025 person-level files and check compatibility and observation counts. | Append and validate when the approved workflow needs it. Otherwise, cite the append portion of the Module 07 assignment and explain why appending does not apply. |
| Merge | Merge regional context and check identifiers, duplicates, matches, and observation counts. | Merge and validate when the approved workflow needs it. Otherwise, cite the merge portion of the Module 07 assignment and explain why merging does not apply. |

For Option B, agree on this evidence plan in the proposal. In the final memo, identify each skill's evidence by filename and script section or Canvas assignment. The cited module evidence must demonstrate competency. Missing or inadequate evidence needs correction before badge completion. If the data or workflow changes, confirm any changes to the approved evidence plan with the instructor in Canvas.

For example, a project using one cross-sectional survey file may need neither an append nor a merge. Its Module 07 assignment can demonstrate both skills. A project that adds contextual variables must demonstrate the merge and its checks in the capstone, even if Module 07 is already complete.

## Minimum Workflow Requirements For Both Options

Your script and documentation should:

1. Run from the project root, with software, package, input-file, and setup requirements documented.
2. Use relative paths and create needed output folders.
3. Start a workflow log or text output record.
4. Import approved source files without changing them manually. Document their source and version.
5. Inspect variable names, types, values, identifiers, units of observation, and missingness before cleaning.
6. Standardize names where needed and perform meaningful, justified cleaning and recoding or variable derivation. Check the results and preserve source meaning.
7. Identify and handle special missing codes when present, summarize missingness, and explain the rules used. If no special codes or missing values are present, document that finding.
8. Create an analytic sample flag before dropping or filtering observations. Explain inclusion rules and track sample sizes, including a justified decision to retain all observations.
9. Perform and validate applicable reshape, append, and merge operations according to the evidence plan above.
10. Run QA checks for required variables, observation counts, identifiers, duplicates, missingness, numeric ranges, and valid categories. Show results and unresolved issues.
11. Save the analysis-ready dataset and generate the codebook, QA evidence, and other outputs from code.
12. Write a workflow memo that explains the decisions and lets another researcher rerun the work.

### Additional Requirements For Option A

The synthetic workflow must import both person-level files, standardize their structure as needed, append them, and check observation counts and respondent-year identifiers. Clean and recode selected variables, convert the teaching data's special missing codes, and create an analytic sample flag. Import the regional context file, check the region identifier, merge it onto the person-level data, and inspect matched and unmatched cases. The provided Stata and R labs illustrate this workflow.

### Applying The Requirements To Option B

Use identifiers, variables, and checks that make sense for the approved data. The synthetic file names, survey years, respondent-year identifier, and regional merge are examples for Option A, not required features of your own project. Explain why each transformation is useful for the stated analytic purpose. Reshape, append, and merge skills that do not apply are assessed through the approved Module 06 or 07 evidence.

## Workflow Memo Prompt

Write a memo, usually 500 to 900 words, that answers:

- Which data option and software pathway did you use?
- What source files, versions, and documentation did you use? For Option B, where are the redistribution terms and instructor approval recorded?
- What is the intended analytic purpose and unit of observation?
- Which script creates the outputs, and how should another researcher obtain the approved inputs and rerun it?
- What variables did you clean, recode, or derive, and how did you check the changes?
- How did you assess and handle missing values?
- What analytic sample flag or restriction did you create, and how did sample size change?
- Where did you demonstrate reshape, append, and merge? For a skill covered by Module 06 or 07, explain why it does not apply to your capstone and identify the exact submitted evidence.
- What checks passed, warned, or identified unresolved issues?
- What limitations remain, and what should a future collaborator do if the outputs need to change?

## Revision Policy

You may submit **two capstone revisions after the initial submission**, for up to three submissions in total. Respond to instructor feedback in Canvas and include a brief note explaining what changed.

Your completion window begins at registration and ends seven calendar days before CU's semester grade-submission deadline. Submit the portfolio and revisions by the cutoff posted in Canvas. Proposal approval does not extend that cutoff. Submit early enough to receive feedback and use your revision opportunities. No peer review is required.

All Module 00–09 assignments remain required badge evidence. A passing capstone grade in Canvas records completion after the required course evidence demonstrates competency. The instructor then notifies the micro registrar to issue the badge. See the [course policies](../syllabus/course-policies.md).
